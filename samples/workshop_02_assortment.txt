Workshop: Group vs. Local Assortment – Decision Rights
Company: Aurora Beauty Group (fictional) – banners Aurora (DK) and Lumi (SE/NO/FI)
Date: 21 October 2026, 13:00–15:00, Stockholm office + Teams
Attendees: Karin Ekström (Group Category Director), Ole Madsen (DK Commercial Lead),
Maja Persson (SE Commercial Lead), Ingrid Solberg (NO Commercial Lead), Aino Virtanen (FI
Commercial Lead), Henrik Dahl (Group Pricing), Rasmus Juul (Group Data & PIM), facilitator
Notes taken by: facilitator

---

Facilitator: The purpose today is decision rights. Who decides what goes on the shelf, where.
We have a draft principle from the operating model work: "Group decides by default, markets
decide by exception." Today we try to make that concrete. Karin?

Karin: Thanks. Today each banner has its own buyers. We buy many of the same brands twice and
negotiate separately. Some suppliers love that, because they can play us against each other.
The proposal from the operating model team is that Group Category Management owns the core
range, roughly eighty percent of SKUs, and markets own a local range of up to twenty percent.

Maja: Twenty percent of SKUs or twenty percent of sales? That's a big difference.

Karin: SKUs. Sales would be more complicated to manage.

Ingrid: For Norway, the local brands are a big part of why customers come to us. Some of
those are Norwegian skincare brands that nobody in Copenhagen has heard of. If they end up in
the "core" bucket and get delisted because they don't perform in Denmark, we lose customers.

Karin: That's exactly what the local range is for.

Ingrid: But twenty percent might not be enough for us.

Ole: Denmark is probably the opposite. We'd use maybe ten percent local.

Facilitator: Can we try to land the principle first, and then deal with the share?

Karin: Proposal: core range is decided by Group Category Management. Local range up to twenty
percent of SKUs per category, decided by the market commercial lead. Can we agree that?

Maja: Yes for the principle.

Aino: Yes.

Ingrid: Yes, with the share reviewed after six months.

Karin: Fine, review after six months. Agreed.

Facilitator: Second topic: new brand listings.

Karin: Today any market can list a new brand. I'd propose any new brand that we'd list in more
than one market goes through the Group range forum. Single-market listings stay local, inside
the twenty percent.

Ole: Who sits on that forum?

Karin: Me, the four commercial leads, and Henrik for pricing. Monthly.

Ole: OK, that works. Agreed from my side.

(others agree)

Facilitator: So the multi-market listing rule is agreed. Third topic, and I know this is the
difficult one: pricing on the local range.

Henrik: From Group Pricing, I'd want pricing on the full range, including local. Otherwise we
can't manage margin.

Maja: But if we own the local range and can't price it, we don't really own it.

Ingrid: Agree with Maja. The whole point is that we know the local market.

Henrik: Then at least give me a guardrail. A margin floor.

Karin: I don't think we'll solve this today. Henrik, Maja, can you work on two options together
and bring them back next session?

Henrik: Sure.

Maja: Yes.

Facilitator: When is the next session?

Karin: Two weeks. 4th of November.

Rasmus: Can I add something? None of this works until we can actually see each other's ranges.
Right now the product data sits in two PIM systems, with different category trees. Karin's team
can't see what's in the Lumi range without asking someone to export it to Excel. We need one
category tree and one product data source before the new decision rights can go live.

Karin: How long?

Rasmus: The category tree, maybe four weeks if the business agrees on it. Merging the PIM
systems is a bigger project, next year. But we could start with a shared reporting view.

Karin: OK. Let's get the shared category tree done first. Rasmus, can you lead that?

Rasmus: I can lead the technical side. I need the category managers to agree the tree.

Karin: I'll get them into a room.

Ole: Practical point. Before we tell the buyers, we need to brief them properly. If they hear
this through rumours, they'll think their jobs are going. Some of the Lumi buyers already
think that.

Maja: Yes, there's real anxiety in my team. Several of them have asked me directly if they'll
be "order-takers for Copenhagen".

Karin: That's not the intention at all, but I understand. We need a proper briefing. Ole, can
you draft the key messages?

Ole: I can draft them, yes.

Facilitator: By when?

Ole: Let me check my calendar and come back.

Aino: And we need the actual RACI written down. Who is responsible, accountable, consulted,
for each step from listing to delisting. Otherwise in three months everyone will remember this
meeting differently.

Karin: Agreed. I'll own the RACI. Draft by the 13th of November.

Henrik: One risk I want on the record: if suppliers find out we're centralising, some of them
will try to lock in better terms with individual markets before the forum is in place.

Karin: Good point. We should tell the key suppliers ourselves, at the right time. Let's think
about who does that.

Facilitator: Wrapping up. Thank you all.

[End of notes]
