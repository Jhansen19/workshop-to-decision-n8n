Workshop: Nordic Replenishment Harmonisation – Session 2
Company: Aurora Beauty Group (fictional) – banners Aurora (DK) and Lumi (SE/NO/FI)
Date: 14 October 2026, 09:00–11:30, Copenhagen hub + Teams
Attendees: Mette Kjær (Group Supply Chain Director), Lars Holm (DK Store Operations),
Sofia Lindqvist (SE Operations), Erik Nygaard (NO Operations), Jukka Salonen (FI Operations),
Anna Berg (Group IT, ERP), Thomas Riis (Group Finance), facilitator
Notes taken by: facilitator (raw, lightly cleaned)

---

Facilitator: Welcome back. Last session we agreed in principle that we want one replenishment
model across the four markets. Today the aim is to land the parameters and the pilot. Mette,
do you want to frame it?

Mette: Yes. Right now Aurora stores in Denmark run daily auto-replenishment from the Brøndby DC,
and the Lumi stores in Sweden, Norway and Finland are on manual orders, twice a week mostly.
Two processes, two ERPs, two sets of KPIs. The ambition is one Group model. Sofia, Lumi-side
stores currently order twice a week?

Sofia: Twice, sometimes three times in peak. Store managers spend about two hours a week on it.
If we go to the DK model of daily auto-replenishment, the stores will love it, but our DC in
Jönköping can't pick that profile yet. It's built for bigger, less frequent orders.

Lars: For what it's worth, when we moved Denmark to daily, store availability went up about two
points in the first quarter. But it took us six months to get the forecasts stable.

Erik: Norway is different anyway. Supplier lead times for some brands are four days longer
because of customs. If you put us on the same parameters we'll be out of stock every Monday.
I'd want a local exception.

Jukka: Finland is closer to Sweden. We could live with the Swedish setup.

Thomas: From a finance side, the question for me is inventory. Daily replenishment should take
working capital out, but only if the safety stock settings are right. If everyone keeps their
own safety stock buffers "just in case", we end up with more stock, not less.

Mette: Fair. OK, let's try to agree the principle. One Group model, daily auto-replenishment,
and Norway gets a lead-time parameter override, not a separate process. Same model, different
parameter. Everyone OK with that?

Erik: As long as the override is mine to set, yes.

Mette: Within limits we agree at Group, yes. OK, agreed.

Facilitator: So that's decision one. Second: safety stock. Thomas, are you proposing a Group
rule?

Thomas: I'd propose safety stock is calculated centrally by Group Supply Chain, using the same
service-level targets per category, and markets can't add manual buffers.

Sofia: I'm not sure the stores will accept that on day one.

Mette: I think it's right. Let's agree it: safety stock is set centrally per category, no
manual buffers. We review it after the pilot. Agreed?

(general agreement)

Anna: Just flagging, none of this works until the item master is cleaned. We have duplicate
SKUs between the two ERPs, different units of measure for the same product, and some
supplier codes that don't match at all. That's maybe eight weeks of work.

Lars: So someone owns the data cleanup?

Anna: I can own it, but I need two people from the business who actually know the ranges.
I can't do it with IT alone.

Mette: OK, Anna owns the item master cleanup. We need to find the two business people. Lars,
Sofia, can you think about who?

Lars: I'll ask around.

Facilitator: Should we put a name against finding those two people?

Mette: Let's come back to it, we don't need to solve it in the room.

Facilitator: OK. Next: the pilot.

Mette: Pilot. Do we pilot in Sweden or Finland first? Jukka?

Jukka: Finland is smaller, safer. Twelve stores, one region. If it goes wrong it's contained.

Sofia: But Sweden is where the volume is, we learn more. And the Jönköping DC is the
bottleneck anyway, so piloting without it doesn't tell us much.

Thomas: Finance would prefer the one with the clearer business case. I haven't seen numbers for
either.

Mette: Let's not decide today. I'll take it to the steering committee on the 4th of November
with both options. Thomas, can you get me a rough business case for both by then?

Thomas: Rough, yes. By the 30th.

Sofia: One more thing on the DC. If we go daily in Sweden, Jönköping needs either more pickers
on the morning shift or a change in the wave planning. I don't have budget for more pickers
this year.

Mette: Noted. Can you work out what it would take? Even a rough estimate.

Sofia: I can do that, but not before mid-November, we're in the middle of Black Friday prep.

Mette: Mid-November is fine.

Erik: Also, the store managers in Norway will need training. They've never used auto-
replenishment. Somebody needs to own the training material, ideally once for the whole Group,
translated.

Mette: Good point. We'll need a training plan. Let's park the owner for that for now.

Facilitator: Before we close, let me read back what I have. Decisions: one Group model with
daily auto-replenishment; Norway lead-time override as a parameter; safety stock set centrally
per category with no manual buffers, reviewed after the pilot. Open: pilot market, which goes to
the steering committee. Actions: Anna owns the item master cleanup; Thomas does business cases
for Sweden and Finland by 30 October. Did I miss anything?

Lars: One thing you didn't say. If the cleanup takes longer than eight weeks, the whole timeline
moves. We should be honest about that with the steering committee.

Mette: Agreed. Let's flag it. Thanks everyone.

[End of notes]
